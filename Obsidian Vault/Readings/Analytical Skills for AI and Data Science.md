# Recommended Reading Roadmap

## Understanding Human Behavior, Incentives, and Business Decisions

Physical levers tend to be domain-specific, but the human levers that affect businesses have been extensively studied by economists, psychologists, and sociologists.

A good starting point is an introductory microeconomics textbook. David Kreps is particularly effective at explaining technical economic concepts to a broad audience.

To understand irrational decision-making and behavioral biases:

- Dan Ariely — *Predictably Irrational* (Harper Perennial)
- Daniel Kahneman — *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (Farrar, Straus and Giroux)

Ariely's book is especially valuable because it presents many examples of interventions that produce surprising results and can help develop more creative ways of thinking about business problems.

For social behavior and interactions:

- Gary Becker & Kevin Murphy — *Social Economics* (Belknap Press)
- Thomas Schelling — *Micromotives and Macrobehavior* (W. W. Norton & Company)

For a more comprehensive and technical treatment of social networks:

- Matthew Jackson — *Social and Economic Networks* (Princeton University Press)

For strategic thinking and game theory:

- Avinash Dixit & Barry Nalebuff — *The Art of Strategy* (W. W. Norton & Company)
- Ken Binmore — *Fun and Games* (D. C. Heath & Co.)
- Ken Binmore — *Playing for Real* (Oxford University Press)

For learning how social scientists use models:

- Scott Page — *The Model Thinker* (Basic Books)

For inventory management and operational decision-making:

- Hillier et al. — *Introduction to Operations Research* (McGraw-Hill Higher Education), particularly Chapter 19 on inventory theory under certainty and uncertainty.
