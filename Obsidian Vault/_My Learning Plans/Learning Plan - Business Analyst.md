### 1. Accounting → How businesses actually work

**Start here.**

1. **Hilton & Platt — _Managerial Accounting_**  
    Cost behavior, margins, budgeting, variance analysis, profitability, operational drivers.
2. **Penman — _Financial Statement Analysis and Security Valuation_**  
    Learn to read financial statements economically, forecast performance, and connect accounting to valuation.
3. **Accounting Information Systems textbook**  
    Understand how transactions become GL entries, invoices, inventory records, payments, controls, etc.  
    _Particularly valuable if you work with business/financial data._
4. **Kaplan & Atkinson — _Advanced Management Accounting_**  
    Advanced cost drivers, ABC, profitability, transfer pricing, performance measurement, incentives.
5. **Kaplan & Anderson — _Time-Driven Activity-Based Costing_**  
    Optional specialization: operational data → resource consumption → cost → profitability.

**Goal:** Be able to explain _why_ revenue, costs, margins, and profitability changed—not merely calculate them.

---

### 2. Economics → How firms make decisions

6. **Kreps — _Microeconomics for Managers_**  
    Incentives, pricing, demand, information, strategic behavior.
7. **Besanko et al. — _Economics of Strategy_**  
    Competition, industry structure, costs, differentiation, vertical integration, strategic behavior.

**Goal:** Understand the economic mechanisms behind business outcomes.

---

### 3. Econometrics → How to learn from data

8. **Angrist & Pischke — _Mastering ’Metrics_**  
    Intuitive introduction to causal inference.
9. **Wooldridge — _Introductory Econometrics_**  
    Systematic foundation: regression, inference, endogeneity, panel data, etc.
10. **Angrist & Pischke — _Mostly Harmless Econometrics_**  
    Move from basic regression toward serious empirical research and identification.
11. **Wooldridge — _Econometric Analysis of Cross Section and Panel Data_**  
    Advanced work with longitudinal/organizational data.
12. **Hernán & Robins — _Causal Inference: What If_**  
    Deepen causal reasoning and research design.

**Goal:** Move from _“these variables correlate”_ to _“here is credible evidence that X caused Y.”_

---

### 4. Advanced methods → Only when needed

**Reference books, not necessarily sequential reading:**

- **Cameron & Trivedi — _Microeconometrics_** → nonlinear models, treatment effects, selection, advanced panel methods.
- **Greene — _Econometric Analysis_** → broad advanced econometrics reference.
- **Hamilton — _Time Series Analysis_** → specialized time-series work.
- **Imbens & Rubin — _Causal Inference_** → advanced causal inference/reference.
