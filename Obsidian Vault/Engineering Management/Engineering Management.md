At fast-growing companies that need to hire several engineering managers, it’s common for tech leads to have the option to move into a manager position. The manager role is different to the software engineer one, and this transition is outside the scope of this book. For advice on navigating the transition into manager, here are books I recommend:

[**Become an Effective Software Engineering Manager**](https://pragprog.com/titles/jsengman/become-an-effective-software-engineering-manager/?ref=blog.pragmaticengineer.com) by James Stanier. A hands-on book for people transitoning to management, starting at a new company or looking to make more of an org-wide impact. [Read my longer book review here](https://blog.pragmaticengineer.com/become-and-effective-software-engineering-manager-my-book-review/), or:

[**An Elegant Puzzle: Systems of Engineering Management**](https://lethain.com/elegant-puzzle/?ref=blog.pragmaticengineer.com) by Will Larson.‎ A hands-on read on _engineering_ management I've picked up. Apart from the book, the Recommended Papers section in the appendix list excellent engineering reads.

[**The Making of a Manager**](https://www.juliezhuo.com/book/manager.html?ref=blog.pragmaticengineer.com) by Julie Zhuo. A guide in going from being a first-time manager to becoming a senior manager in an organization. A refreshingly honest tone, and all the experiences and stories come directly from [Julie Zhuo](https://www.juliezhuo.com/?ref=blog.pragmaticengineer.com), who worked her way from individual contributor to VP of Design at Facebook.

[**The Manager's Path**](https://learning.oreilly.com/library/view/the-managers-path/9781491973882/?ref=blog.pragmaticengineer.com) by Camille Fournier. A concise guide for managers, from tech lead, through line managers, managers of managers, all the way to CTO.
